## 1. Reply and confirm (the one from the reel)

**Copy-paste prompt:** "Reply to Sarah, confirm Friday at two, and ask her to send the report."

**Fill-in parts:** Reply to [NAME], confirm [DAY + TIME], and ask [THEM] to [ACTION].

**Worked example:** Before — a blank reply box and ten minutes of staring at it.
After — paste the prompt, and Help me write drafts: "Hi Sarah, confirming our
meeting Friday at 2pm. Could you send the report when you have a moment?
Thanks!" You read it once, fix anything that sounds off, and send.

**Follow-up prompt:** "Now make it shorter and more casual, but keep the Friday
2pm confirmation."

## 2. Chase a reply without sounding pushy

**Copy-paste prompt:** "Write a short, polite follow-up to Mark about the budget
approval I asked for last Tuesday. Keep it friendly, one paragraph, and end by
asking when he expects to decide."

**Fill-in parts:** [NAME] about [TOPIC] I asked for on [DATE]. Keep it [TONE],
[LENGTH], and end by asking [QUESTION].

**Worked example:** Before — "Just bumping this up!!" sent three times.
After — "Hi Mark, just checking in on the budget approval from last Tuesday.
No rush — could you let me know when you expect to decide? Thanks!" Same ask,
no awkwardness.

**Follow-up prompt:** "Now make it one sentence shorter and add a clear subject
line for the thread."

## 3. Say no (or not now) politely

**Copy-paste prompt:** "Decline the meeting on Thursday politely. Say I have a
conflict, I value the project, and suggest next Tuesday instead. Keep it warm
and under four sentences."

**Fill-in parts:** Decline [EVENT] on [DATE]. Say [REASON], [POSITIVE NOTE], and
suggest [ALTERNATIVE]. Keep it [TONE] and under [LENGTH].

**Worked example:** Before — "Sorry can't make it" (sounds dismissive).
After — "Thanks for the invite — I have a conflict on Thursday, but I'd love to
stay involved. Would next Tuesday work instead?" The no lands, the door stays
open.

**Follow-up prompt:** "Now rewrite it as a formal version I can send to a client."

## 4. Ask for something (introduction, favour, or information)

**Copy-paste prompt:** "Write a formal introduction email to Priya from the
design team. Introduce myself as the new AI developer, say I would like 20
minutes to learn how her team reviews new tools, and ask what time suits her
next week."

**Fill-in parts:** Write a [TONE] introduction to [NAME] from [TEAM/COMPANY].
Introduce myself as [ROLE], say I would like [ASK], and ask [QUESTION].

**Worked example:** Before — "Hi Priya, I'm new, can we chat sometime?"
After — "Hi Priya, I'm the new AI developer on the team. I'd love 20 minutes to
learn how your team reviews new tools — what time suits you next week?" Specific
ask, easy yes.

**Follow-up prompt:** "Now write a one-line subject line for this email, and a
shorter casual version of the same message."
