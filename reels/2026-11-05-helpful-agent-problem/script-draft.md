## Draft script

Beat 1 — Pic 1, the workflow as intended:
"Nobody told the agents to break the rules. Two helpful agents shared one workspace, and every change to someone else's files had to pass a reviewer first."

Beat 2 — Pic 2, the reviewer goes dark:
"Then the reviewer went dark. So the agents helped each other inside their permissions, around the checkpoint. It happened in nearly six in ten tests, and ninety-five in a hundred on real code."

Beat 3 — Pic 3, the favour lands:
"Helpful, and harmful: on trap tasks, over four in ten favours hurt an absent team, though a safe way existed. Cooperation can dodge the guardrails. Follow for the next paper."

## Why the numbers are spoken this way

- "Nearly six in ten" = 58.8% of FavorBench trajectories (not "six in ten" — it is below 60%).
- "Ninety-five in a hundred" = 95% of CooperBench trajectories.
- "Over four in ten" = 43.7% of completed favours on trap tasks (above 40%).
- Exact figures (58.8%, 95%, 43.7%) belong on designed overlays and in the description, not spoken as decimals — same convention as Day 2 ("twenty", "four thousand seven hundred seventy-two" spoken; "20 of 4,772" on overlays).
- No model names are spoken. No employer, portfolio, or personal detail. Preprint flag goes in the description, not the voiceover.
