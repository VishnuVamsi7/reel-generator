## Treatment — what "Remotion camera" means, and what it does NOT mean

- **Motion = a camera view-window travelling inside the intact image only** (zoompan-style). The source PNG is never altered: it is scaled and translated as one whole frame. The output frame is 720x1280 throughout.
- **CUTS between beats only. No morphs, no cross-dissolves that smear one image into the next.** Pic 1, 2, 3 are different compositions; a morph would break the artwork.
- **FORBIDDEN, as in Phase 1:** segmentation, masking, rotation, cropping the artwork apart, sprites, cutting elements out and re-laying them, recreating any background, or redrawing any block, funnel, gate, arrow, or set-aside piece. A camera window that temporarily shows only part of the image is NOT "cropping the artwork apart" — the artwork itself is untouched and every element stays in its original relative position — but the window must never be framed so that a story-critical detail is left outside it at the moment it matters (see the per-beat end-frame guarantees below).
- **Kit:** NOT in this build, as in Phase 1, for the same written reason: a foreground character would cover the gaps, the tilted block, the gate's approval marker, and the set-aside pieces — the exact details the muted test depends on. The supplied figures are the host; the camera move is the host motion. Character track on every beat: none (image-led build).
- **No generation prompt exists for this treatment.** There is no video model to prompt. The generator/editor works from the camera parameters in the table below only. If a parameter cannot be executed as written, it comes back to the director — no silent substitution.

## Coordinate convention — read this before building

- Source image coordinates: 720 wide × 1280 tall, origin at top-left, x right, y down. Image centre is (360, 640).
- **Scale** = magnification of the source. The camera viewport (the rectangle of source shown in the 720x1280 output) is **720 ÷ scale wide × 1280 ÷ scale tall**.
- **Centre (cx, cy)** = the source point at the centre of the viewport. Offset dx = cx − 360, dy = cy − 640.
- **Clamping rule (the editor must enforce this):** the viewport must stay inside the source. So cx must stay in [viewport-width ÷ 2, 720 − viewport-width ÷ 2] and cy in [viewport-height ÷ 2, 1280 − viewport-height ÷ 2]. Every end centre below is at or inside its clamp limit — do not push it further, or the output will show blank edges / a shifted frame.
- **Easing:** easeInOutCubic on both scale and centre, over the full beat duration, unless the editor flags a Remotion-native equivalent. No linear pan (it reads mechanical), no ease that parks the camera still for the first or last 2 seconds (that reads as a freeze and risks the dead-air check).
- **Overlays are a designed layer on the finished 720x1280 output, not part of the camera.** Place them only in the blank zones below; never let an overlay sit on a story detail, whatever the camera is doing.
  - Top blank zone (output canvas): y ≈ 40–300. Bottom blank zone: y ≈ 1050–1220. The artwork band sits roughly y ≈ 500–970 in source coordinates (Pic 3's arrow dips to ≈ y 970; Pic 1/2 artwork ends ≈ y 890), so these zones stay blank in every start and end frame below.

## Director note — honest muted-read judgement (for QC to test, not assume)

Camera-only motion is the known Day 1 v1 limitation, and this re-issue does not fix it — it only frames it as well as camera travel allows. The camera moves continuously in every beat, so no beat should trip the dead-air freeze rule on paper, but **continuous reframing of a static artwork is not the same as action on screen.** QC must run the muted-no-captions test (sound off, captions off, designed overlays still on) and judge these predictions, not take them as passes:

- **Beat 1 — READS MUTED (gist).** Complete grid → funnel squeeze → broken panel is a visible journey, and "Clean in. Warped out." names it. What muted loses: the scale of the hiding (20 of 4,772) exists only in the voiceover. A muted viewer gets "something got warped by the formatter," not "the printout hid 4,752 documents." That is a gist pass, a specifics fail.
- **Beat 2 — READS MUTED ONLY VIA THE OVERLAY; RISKS DEPENDING ON CAPTIONS for the claim.** Gaps + glowing marker together in one frame, plus "Approved anyway.," lets a muted viewer infer "broken thing got approved." But the marker itself is abstract — a glowing teal/amber dot, not a tick or a stamp — so without the overlay word "Approved" the approval story is weak, and without captions/voiceover the entire test result (three kinds of error, all ninety verdicts flipped) is invisible. If QC finds muted viewers reading the marker as decoration, Beat 2 is caption-dependent and must be reported as such, not passed on the overlay alone.
- **Beat 3 — WEAKEST MUTED READ; HIGHEST CAPTION DEPENDENCE. Judge this beat hardest.** What is visible muted: an open filter → travel along an amber arrow → a complete grid, with three small faded pieces set aside bottom-right and the overlay "Say what's left out." What is NOT visible muted: the mechanism (rewriting a printout to state limits/caps and how to handle them — no rewriting is shown, the arrow does not depict it), the checker (no gate/checker appears in Pic 3 at all, so "the checker catches the error" has no image), and the set-aside pieces are small and sit at the frame's bottom-right corner, easy to miss at end scale 1.30. The beat is also the longest single camera move (15.4s); a slow left-to-right rise over that duration risks reading as a slow drift over a still image even though the frame is technically travelling. Predicted verdict: **fix gist visible, fix mechanism + checker payoff caption-dependent.** If the muted test confirms that, QC should report Beat 3 as caption-dependent rather than claiming a muted pass — and any future fix (not in this build) would need a visual for the checker returning, not a longer pan.
- **Overall:** expect a muted viewer to follow problem → approval → fix at gist level with overlays on, and to miss the numbers, the 90/90 result, and the rewrite mechanism without captions. That is the honest ceiling of the camera-only treatment, and it matches the Day 1 v1 lesson rather than contradicting it.
