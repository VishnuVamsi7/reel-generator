Do different models learn the same picture? This paper turns that idea into a theorem: when two views show one scene, separately trained models align, up to a single rotation.

Good views are the lever. Two views of one scene needed forty-two times fewer samples than next-token prediction for the same alignment, and a small text model beat models up to seventy-two billion parameters at matching vision.

Honest limits: the theorem holds under stated assumptions, on the paper's own alignment benchmark — not general capability. Preprint, not peer-reviewed.
