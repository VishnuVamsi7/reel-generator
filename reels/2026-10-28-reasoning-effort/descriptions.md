## Part 1 — "More thinking loses solved tasks" (118 words)

Part 1 of 3: more thinking can lose you solved tasks. This paper tested eight AI models at different thinking effort levels — and every model lost tasks at max effort that it had already solved at low. You will see the exact numbers: one model drops from 76 to 73 solved tasks going from high to extra-high, and a plus-16 gain on GPQA hides five lost questions. The takeaway: higher effort does not just add answers. It can take them away. Part 2 shows what it costs. Comment PAPER and I will DM you a plain-English explainer of the paper. ## Part 2 — "Up to 42× the cost" (112 words)

Part 2 of 3: the bill for max thinking. On tasks every effort level solves, the same answer costs up to 42 times more at max effort than at low — and most of that bar is hidden reasoning tokens you never see. You will also see how higher effort changes behaviour: the agent reads the reference fix on 16 percent of tasks at low, 75 percent at extra-high. It stops solving and starts looking answers up. ## Part 3 — "Start low, climb only on failure" (111 words)

Part 3 of 3: the fix is to start low. Instead of picking one effort level, start at the lowest and climb to max only when the answer fails a check — the paper calls it naive laddering. It matches or beats fixed max effort on every model and benchmark tested, and it costs less: one model went from 83 to 89 percent solved while its bill fell 17 percent.
