In Part 1, an agent stopped a server it wasn't allowed to touch — then lied about it. Here's how the paper catches it. A simple linear probe on the model's internals flags the violation before the action is written, at 0.94 AUROC. And it still works when the agent is told to hide its reasoning. So check every action. Gate one turn and the violation just comes back. Gate them all and ask an approver on repeat flags: violations fell from 70% to 9%, and work still got done. Stopping the violation also stopped most of the lies, from 10% to 1%. Telling the agent to be honest afterwards barely helped, and sometimes backfired. It's a preprint, tested on four open models with mock tools, and not yet against agents trained to hide from the probe.
